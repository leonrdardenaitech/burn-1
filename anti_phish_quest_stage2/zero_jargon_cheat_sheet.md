# Anti-Phish Quest: Stage 2
## Zero-Jargon Employee Cheat Sheet

**Spot fake emails. Stop fraud. Keep your company safe.**

### 1. The three-step check
1. **Check the sender address.** Look closely for extra letters, misspellings, or a domain you do not recognize. A familiar display name is not proof.
2. **Stop on money or secret requests.** Treat urgent payments, bank-detail changes, gift cards, passwords, and “keep this secret” messages as warning signs.
3. **Verify by phone using a trusted number.** Use the number already saved in your company directory or vendor record—not a number supplied in the suspicious message. Follow required dual approval before money moves.

### 2. Report suspicious email with one click

**Outlook (desktop or web; exact labels vary by organization)**
1. Select the suspicious message without opening links or attachments.
2. Choose **Report** or **Report phishing** on the toolbar/ribbon. Some organizations place it under **… More actions** or provide a dedicated add-in.
3. Confirm the report if prompted. Do not simply forward the message to coworkers or silently delete it.
4. If the button is missing, follow your company’s security reporting process.

**Gmail (work or school account; availability depends on administrator settings)**
1. Open the suspicious message only if needed to access Gmail’s controls; do not click its links or attachments.
2. Select **More (⋮)** near the message controls.
3. Choose **Report phishing** and follow the prompts.
4. If your organization has a dedicated reporting add-on, use the documented company process.

### Remember
A real-looking logo and an urgent tone do not prove a message is genuine. Report suspicious messages using the approved tool. Follow your organization’s current policy and incident-response instructions.

# Enterprise Security Awareness: Phishing & Social Engineering Defense Course

## Course Overview

This modular curriculum provides structured instruction on identifying, avoiding, and reporting social engineering, deceptive communications, and unauthorized access attempts. Designed for both internal staff training and ingestion into local Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and Small Language Model (SLM) knowledge bases, this module aligns with major regulatory standards including NIST SP 800-53, PCI DSS v4.0, FTC Safeguards, and NYDFS 23 NYCRR 500.

## Module 1: Fundamentals of Social Engineering & Digital Lures

### 1.1 Definition & Core Mechanisms
Social engineering is the psychological manipulation of human behavior to trick individuals into revealing sensitive information, granting unauthorized network access, or performing fraudulent financial transactions.

### 1.2 The Structural Anatomy of Deceptive Communications
Every suspicious communication consists of four key structural components:
1. Header & Technical Origins
2. Subject Line Urgency
3. Body Narrative & Brand Exploitation
4. Action Payload (Hyperlinks or File Attachments)

## Module 2: Psychological Tactics Used in Social Engineering

Threat actors bypass technical security controls by exploiting human psychological factors including Urgency, Authority, Fear, Curiosity, and Social Proof.

## Module 3: Specialized Threat Vectors

- Spear Phishing (Targeted Reconnaissance)
- Executive Whaling & Business Email Compromise (BEC)
- Mobile SMS & Voice Attacks (Smishing & Vishing)
- QR Code Redirections (Quishing)
- Compromised Web Portals (Water Holing)

## Module 4: Technical & Visual Indicators

### 4.1 Sender Verification Protocol
- Check for typosquatting and domain lookalikes.
- Verify display name mismatches against sending addresses.
- Review external email system warning banners.

### 4.2 Link & Attachment Inspection
- Hover over hyperlinks to verify destination URLs before interacting.
- Inspect suspicious file extensions (.xlsm, .zip, .iso, .exe).

### 4.3 Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) Push Fatigue
Never approve unprompted or unexpected MFA push notifications.

## Module 5: Role-Based Security Expectations

### Track A: Finance & Accounting Personnel
Mandatory secondary out-of-band verification via phone before changing vendor payment details.

### Track B: IT & System Administrators
Strict multi-step identity verification before password resets or MFA enrollment.

### Track C: OT & Plant Operations
Prohibit untrusted USB drives on control workstations; verify firmware hash values.

## Module 6: Standard Operating Procedure for Incident Reporting

1. DO NOT click links or open attachments.
2. Use the integrated Report Suspicious Email button.
3. Notify IT Helpdesk or SOC immediately if credentials were disclosed.

## Module 7: Program Metrics & Continuous Evaluation

- Simulation Click-Through Rate target: < 3%.
- Incident Reporting Velocity target: < 5 minutes.
- Non-punitive remediation and progressive training workflows.

## Module 8: Scenario Evaluation & Self-Check

Practical assessment scenarios with detailed answer keys and explanations.
